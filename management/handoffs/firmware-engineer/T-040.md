## Findings
- **DOC-1 (FIXED)**: `blink()` used `delay()`, so selecting a scale, recalibrating or the boot "ready" signal froze touch scanning, the button, the pot and the USB MIDI task. This took up to 1080 ms (scale 6).
  - Before: e4668d4 `FrutaMIDI.ino:153-158` (`digitalWrite(...HIGH); delay(ms); …`), called from `nextScale` (line 210), `recalibrate` (204) and `setup` (293). In the host blocking model, a 200 ms touch during the scale-6 signal produced no note.
  - After: `fm::Blinker`, ticked by `led.level_or()` in both LED writes of `loop()`. The count and the 90/60/120 ms on/off values are unchanged (test `test_patterns_unchanged_from_e4668d4`). The touch produces ON/OFF at the exact times. `test_no_blocking_blink_left` checks that the only `delay()` left is the 10 ms USB re-attach.
  - Side effects of the fix, all intended:
    - (a) A button press during a blink is now honoured, and the blink restarts with the new count. Before, such a press was lost.
    - (b) While a blink runs, the LED shows the pattern instead of the touch state. Before, nothing else ran at all.
    - (c) Each half-period can now be up to one loop period longer than 90 ms. Before, it was 90 ms plus the `digitalWrite` overhead.
- **Test defects I found and fixed during the work** (not firmware bugs):
  - One wrong expected value in my own clamp table (126 written as 127). Removed; the case is covered correctly by `test_clamp_one_below_max`.
  - Mutant M9 (`now >= since + interval`, not wrap-safe) first survived. I added `test_elapsed_is_wrap_safe`, and M9 is now killed.
- **Observations, not changed** (no behaviour change requested):
  - (1) The aftertouch change filter (|Δ| ≥ 2) never sends a final 1 → 0 drop before note-off.
  - (2) Pad debounce is a 15 ms lockout after each accepted change, not a stable-for-N-ms filter.
  - (3) A long press still blocks for the duration of `startSensors()`, which is the MPR121 reset/autoconfig over I2C and has not been measured.

## Assumptions
- The 10-bit ADC on all targets. SOURCE for RP2040 (`_readBits = 10`); AVR is 10-bit native.
- Button bounce < 10 ms, and a loop period well under 15 ms. Both need hardware (listed in `coverage.md`).
- The C++ harness `loopsim` copies the call order of `loop()`. `test_sketch_uses_headers.py` guards that the sketch calls the same functions, but not their order. I reviewed the order by hand.
