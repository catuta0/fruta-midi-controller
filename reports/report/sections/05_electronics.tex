\subsection{ESD flashover around the series resistor and pulse rating (CR-002)}
\label{sec:esd}
Sources: \ev{reports/electronics/analysis.md} \S5,
\ev{management/handoffs/electronics-engineer/T-060.md}, raw outputs
\nolinkurl{calc_flashover.out}, \nolinkurl{calc_pulse_rated.out},
\nolinkurl{check_gap.out} and \nolinkurl{check_gap_old_86866be.out} in
\ev{sim/electronics/calc/}. Change request: \ev{changes/CR-002.md}; decisions
ADR-019.

\paragraph{Mechanism.} During a contact discharge almost the whole IEC voltage
appears across the series resistor, because the pin structure holds the ELE side
near 0\,V (\lbl{ASSUMPTION}):
$V_R = V \cdot R/(R + 330\,\Omega)$ = 3636\,V at 4\,kV and 7273\,V at 8\,kV
(\lbl{CALCULATION}). Two things can then defeat the resistor: an arc across the air
gap between its two ends, which leaves the pin with only the 330\,$\Omega$ source
(12.1\,A at 4\,kV and 24.2\,A at 8\,kV, against 1.10\,A and 2.20\,A through $R$,
\lbl{CALCULATION}); or a resistor body not rated for that voltage.

\paragraph{Correction of an earlier claim.} The v1.1 analysis stated that ``each
path still has its series R''. That was wrong for the gap across the resistor
itself: with the vertical DIN0204 footprint (pads 1.6\,mm on 2.54\,mm pitch) the
ELE$x$--E$x$ copper gap was 0.94\,mm (\lbl{MEASUREMENT}, design file of 86866be),
and the vertical lead ran about 1\,mm from the opposite end cap
(\lbl{ASSUMPTION}). At an assumed 3\,kV/mm breakdown that gap flashes over at about
2.8\,kV, below the 3.64\,kV across $R$ (\lbl{CALCULATION}, CR-002). The resistor
(Yageo MFR-12, 400\,V maximum overload, \lbl{SOURCE} EE-S14b) was also not rated
for the pulse.

\paragraph{Breakdown sweep and gap rule.} Every breakdown model is an
\lbl{ASSUMPTION}; the constants are stated in \nolinkurl{calc_flashover.py}. The
IEC 60664-1 values were recalled, not re-read (EE-S25), and must be checked against
the standard.

\subsection{Safety statement}
Each PCB electrode has a \qty{3.3}{\kilo\ohm} pulse-rated series resistor (Vishay
MBE04140C3301FC100) lying flat, with at least 13.04\,mm between the copper on its two
sides (\lbl{MEASUREMENT}, design file). Its protection against IEC 61000-4-2
contact discharge at $\pm$4\,kV is an estimate resting on \lbl{ASSUMPTION}s (the pin
limit is inferred from HBM 2\,kV; the breakdown models and the pulse-rating proxy
are assumed), not a pass (review finding F-002
\ev{reports/review/72f67f0/findings.md}). Only an ESD-gun test can close it. At
8\,kV the resistor rating is exceeded (R-006). The design is not evaluated for
touching while connected to mains-powered equipment (ADR-003, ADR-012, ADR-019).
