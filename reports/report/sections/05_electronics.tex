\subsection{ESD flashover around the series resistor and pulse rating (CR-002)}
\label{sec:esd}
Sources: \ev{reports/electronics/analysis.md} \S5,
\ev{management/handoffs/electronics-engineer/T-060.md},
\ev{management/handoffs/electronics-engineer/T-073.md}, raw outputs
\nolinkurl{calc_flashover.out}, \nolinkurl{calc_pulse_rated.out},
\nolinkurl{check_gap.out}, \nolinkurl{check_gap_old_86866be.out},
\nolinkurl{check_gap_old_1247c44.out}, \nolinkurl{calc_gnd_bounce.out} and
\nolinkurl{calc_mechanical.out} in \ev{sim/electronics/calc/}. Change request:
\ev{changes/CR-002.md}; decisions ADR-019 and, after the re-review (Section~\ref{sec:cr002-review}),
ADR-020.

\paragraph{Mechanism.} During a contact discharge almost the whole IEC voltage
appears across the series resistor, because the pin structure holds the ELE side
near 0\,V (\lbl{ASSUMPTION}):
$V_R = V \cdot R/(R + 330\,\Omega)$ = 3636\,V at 4\,kV and 7273\,V at 8\,kV
(\lbl{CALCULATION}). Two things can then defeat the resistor: an arc across the air
gap between its two ends, which leaves the pin with only the 330\,$\Omega$ source
(12.1\,A at 4\,kV and 24.2\,A at 8\,kV, against 1.10\,A and 2.20\,A through $R$,
\lbl{CALCULATION}); or a resistor body not rated for that voltage.

\paragraph{Correction of an earlier claim.} The v1.1 analysis stated that ``each
path still has its series R''. That was wrong for the gap across the resistor
itself: with the vertical DIN0204 footprint (pads 1.6\,mm on 2.54\,mm pitch) the
ELE$x$--E$x$ copper gap was 0.94\,mm (\lbl{MEASUREMENT}, design file of 86866be),
and the vertical lead ran about 1\,mm from the opposite end cap
(\lbl{ASSUMPTION}). At an assumed 3\,kV/mm breakdown that gap flashes over at about
2.8\,kV, below the 3.64\,kV across $R$ (\lbl{CALCULATION}, CR-002). The resistor
(Yageo MFR-12, 400\,V maximum overload, \lbl{SOURCE} EE-S14b) was also not rated
for the pulse.

\paragraph{Breakdown sweep and gap rule.} Every breakdown model is an
\lbl{ASSUMPTION}; the constants are stated in \nolinkurl{calc_flashover.py}. The
IEC 60664-1 values were recalled, not re-read (EE-S25), and must be checked against
the standard.

\emph{Pulse energy (corrected in T-073, finding C2-003).} The 1.2/50\,\textmu s
rating pulse puts $(V_\mathrm{pk}^2/R)\int (V/V_\mathrm{pk})^2\,dt$ =
4083$^2$/3300 $\cdot$ 36.06\,\textmu s $\approx$ \textbf{182\,mJ} into 3.3\,k$\Omega$
(\lbl{CALCULATION}; ``Rating pulse energy: integral (V/Vpk)\^{}2 dt = 36.06 us
\ldots{} -> 182 mJ into 3k3 at 4083 V''). The T-060 value of about 364\,mJ used
$\int V\,dt$ and was about twice too high. Below its knee the MBE 0414 chart has a
log-log slope of 0.496, i.e.\ constant $V^2/R$: at 3.3\,k$\Omega$ the rating is
\textbf{energy-limited}; above the knee at \textbf{5073\,$\Omega$} it is
voltage-limited at a plateau of \textbf{5054\,V} (\lbl{CALCULATION} from the
\lbl{SOURCE} chart). One 4\,kV IEC strike puts 1.09\,mJ and 3636\,V into $R$:
167$\times$ below the rating-pulse energy and 1.39$\times$ below the plateau; at
8\,kV, 4.36\,mJ and 7273\,V exceed both the 3.3\,k$\Omega$ chart value and the
plateau (\lbl{CALCULATION}). The 3.3\,k$\Omega$ chart value 4083\,V stays the
acceptance limit (conservative); verdicts unchanged: PASS at 4\,kV, FAIL at 8\,kV.
Taking the 1.2/50\,\textmu s rating as a proxy for the $\approx$0.54\,\textmu s IEC
pulse is an \lbl{ASSUMPTION}. The MBE 0414 keeps the 3.3\,k$\Omega$ 1\,\% value, so the
sensitivity analysis above is unchanged (ADR-019 D-1).

\paragraph{Mounting metal (T-073, finding C2-009).} A metal screw head, washer or
spacer (radius 2.8--3.5\,mm, \lbl{ASSUMPTION}) at a mounting hole can bridge two
caps; an arc ELE$x$ $\rightarrow$ metal $\rightarrow$ E$x$ is counted as the sum of
the two gaps (\lbl{ASSUMPTION}). At the old H3 (95, 48)\,mm the bypass sum was
1.94\,mm: FAIL against 3.5\,mm (\ev{sim/electronics/calc/check_gap_old_1247c44.out}).
H3 moved to (96, 38)\,mm, where it faces only the ELE11 cap: worst bypass over
H1--H4 ``PASS (e) floating-metal bypass worst 15.36 mm vs rule 3.5 mm (4 kV); PASS
vs 7.5 mm (8 kV)'' (\lbl{CALCULATION}). Nylon hardware at H3 is still recommended in
the assembly guide.

\paragraph{E$x$ $\leftrightarrow$ GND 1.40\,mm kept as an intentional spark gap
(T-073, finding C2-005, ADR-020).} Every J2 electrode pad is 1.40\,mm
(\lbl{MEASUREMENT}, design file) from the GND edge track, below the breakdown
distance of every model at 3.64\,kV (0.67--3.33\,mm), so a 4\,kV strike most likely
arcs to GND there. That keeps the strike off R$x$ and the MPR121 pin; moving the
track away would put the full 1.10\,A into the pin through R$x$. The arc current
then raises the board ground. GND track lengths (\lbl{MEASUREMENT}, design file):
terminal GND to Pico pin 23 24.04\,mm, module GND pin to Pico pin 23 31.36\,mm,
shared section 4.72\,mm. With the IEC 4\,kV current (\lbl{SOURCE} EE-S16, recalled
from memory: first peak 17.2\,A at the upper tolerance, rise 0.6--1.0\,ns), track
inductance 0.7--1.0\,nH/mm and socket plus header pin 3--10\,nH (\lbl{ASSUMPTION}),
the module-GND to Pico-GND bounce is \textbf{87--339\,V for $\approx$1\,ns}, about
4.5\,V after the first peak; through R15/R16 (220\,$\Omega$, then the module's
BSS138 level shifters) that is \textbf{$\le$1.53\,A} and $\le$0.92\,nC per
I\textsuperscript{2}C line (\lbl{CALCULATION} on \lbl{ASSUMPTION}s; ``Worst case:
module GND - Pico GND bounce 339 V for \textasciitilde1 ns'',
\ev{sim/electronics/calc/calc_gnd_bounce.out}). The RP2040 HBM 4\,kV rating
(\lbl{SOURCE} EE-S28) is stated for digital FT pins; no HBM value was found for
GP26/GP27, which carry I\textsuperscript{2}C1 and are ADC-capable (NOT CONFIRMED,
escalated). Residual risk: an I\textsuperscript{2}C pin upset or latch-up from the
$\approx$1\,ns bounce. It is an estimate, not a measurement; the ESD test watches
for an arc to the GND track and for I\textsuperscript{2}C errors and resets after
each strike (V-10, V-25).

\paragraph{8\,kV deferred.} At 8\,kV the copper gaps meet the 7.5\,mm rule but the
end caps do not, and the
selected resistor does not hold 7.27\,kV (4.08\,kV rating): FAIL, and the pin current
of 2.20\,A exceeds the inferred 1.33\,A limit by 1.65$\times$
(\lbl{CALCULATION} on \lbl{ASSUMPTION}). The scope stays $\pm$4\,kV (REQ-023,
ADR-019 D-1). The LSR37 (same DIN0414 footprint, 12\,kV rated) is a later option;

\subsection{Safety statement}
Each PCB electrode has a \qty{3.3}{\kilo\ohm} pulse-rated series resistor (Vishay
MBE04140C3301FC100) lying flat, with at least 13.04\,mm between the copper on its two
sides (\lbl{MEASUREMENT}, design file); on the component side the end-cap gap is
estimated at 3.61\,mm or more for a hand-fitted part within $\pm$0.25\,mm
(\lbl{CALCULATION} on an \lbl{ASSUMPTION}ed cap length, T-073). The J2 electrode
pads sit 1.40\,mm from the GND edge track, an intentional spark gap that diverts a
strike to ground (ADR-020). Its protection against IEC 61000-4-2
contact discharge at $\pm$4\,kV is an estimate resting on \lbl{ASSUMPTION}s (the pin
limit is inferred from HBM 2\,kV; the breakdown models and the pulse-rating proxy
are assumed), not a pass (review finding F-002
\ev{reports/review/72f67f0/findings.md}). Only an ESD-gun test can close it. At
8\,kV the resistor rating is exceeded (R-006). The design is not evaluated for
touching while connected to mains-powered equipment (ADR-003, ADR-012, ADR-019).
